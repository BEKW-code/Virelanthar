0. Fast translator's answer
Can Zoé translate Virelanthar today? Partly. A small number of expressions have stable, documented English glosses, while a much larger collection of older poetic forms has only draft, historically asserted meanings. There is no sufficiently validated word-by-word lexicon and grammar to translate an arbitrary new Virelanthar paragraph with confidence. The right approach is source-first translation with uncertainty notes, and, if creating new bilingual lyrics, to agree and record new words before singing them.

Evidence tiers used throughout
A — User-established: Explicitly supplied by Keth’ar in prior conversation and preserved consistently in project context.
B — Master-preserved: Exact form and English meaning present in the project master reference, but not proof of complete compositional grammar.
C — Historical assistant draft: Form and gloss recoverable from an earlier assistant output. Useful research material, not confirmed canon.
D — Historical structural proposal: Described grammar or translation rule, not a fully implemented or verified grammar.
X — Unknown/conflicted: Contradictory, incomplete, or unglossed. Never translate with unwarranted certainty.
The document attempts no new lexical inventions. Every language form below has a previously recorded lineage, or is clearly labeled an unverified example from the most recent generated song verse.

7. Grammar: what has been described vs. what is actually known
7.1 Historical conceptual grammar (D)
Sound-as-meaning: Sonic texture and rhythm carry metaphor and emphasis alongside lexical meaning.
S–V–Resonance–Intent: A proposed structure uses subject, verb, a resonant/emotive phrase, and an intent or effect. This is an organizing idea, not a proven general syntactic rule.
Echo-based agreement: Repeated or harmonized sounds may indicate relation, belonging or rhetorical intensification. The exact triggers, affected classes and required endings are missing.
Echo-modifier suffixes: Historical plans described semantic suffixes; paradigms and meanings are not settled.
Candidate prefix inventory: `a-`, `ta-`, `vi-` were discussed as initiation, transformation, internalization in earlier conceptual planning. No verified conjugated sentences demonstrate this assignment consistently.
High / Low Speech: ceremonial dense register versus direct, embodied register. Earlier Low Speech examples sometimes mix English and invented Virelanthar; this is code-switching, not evidence of a valid fully Virelanthar grammar.
Vowel/rhythm harmony: claimed as a design direction without a formal harmony table, permitted syllable structure or phrase stress grammar.
7.2 Historical Low Speech example (not a grammar model)
> `Thal vi'ren. Sol come. Flame hold. No dark cross.`
This was presented historically as an earthy, plain-spoken example. It includes English tokens, and `Thal` / `vi'ren` are not independently glossed. Treat it as a performance mood board, not a translation template.

8. Pronunciation / singing guidance — limited, honest
The language name was earlier approximated as vee-REH-lahn-thar. Three everyday draft words have phonetic cues in §3. Nothing more precise has been validated as universal orthographic law.
For singing:
Keep accents and apostrophes exactly as written on the lyric sheet.
Ask the composer which syllable takes musical stress where no guide exists. Don't assume apostrophe always means a glottal stop, a pause, or a morpheme break.
Compose to natural breath groups, then use music phrasing to test whether the syllables can be performed consistently.
For bilingual captioning, use literal/gloss translation only when supported and separately label free poetic interpretation.
A Suno bracket like `[MALE / VIRELANTHAR — rough low baritone]` describes the voice and language but does not prove the lyrics are grammatical Virelanthar.
No IPA inventory or numerical phonetic transcription is claimed.

10. Practical translation protocol for Zoé
When translating Virelanthar → English or French
Preserve the exact source including accents, punctuation and case.
Match whole expressions first against the A/B stable section (§§2, 9). These are the only reliable full glosses.
Check the C historical dictionary and phrasebook for candidates, without treating draft status as final.
Do not split compounds by intuition. Similar-looking morphemes may be coincidences or decorative sound patterns.
Write separate fields for literal verified content, historical draft gloss, and free poetic reading.
For unglossed words, use [meaning not established]; do not invent a French or English definition.
Flag any numeral as unresolved; identify the competing set if it matters.
Invite the author to confirm the intended meaning and the stressed syllables before publishing a definitive bilingual subtitle.
Record the final accepted form, gloss, pronunciation, date and author; treat this as an addition rather than retroactively declaring an older form canonical.
When composing English/French → Virelanthar
First write exactly what the singer means in plain language, independent of the conlang sound.
Reuse A/B accepted whole phrases where the meaning fits.
For additional concepts, create an explicit proposal row with source phrase, proposed Virelanthar form, English gloss, French gloss, pronunciation, rationale, and acceptance status.
Do not manufacture a word-by-word translation from the current table; the grammar is not sufficient.
If Keth’ar accepts a proposed word or form, record that date and decision and update this file in a new version.
Preserve Zoé’s Vaeltharyn entirely separately; let the languages answer each other rather than creating a hybrid grammar by accident.
