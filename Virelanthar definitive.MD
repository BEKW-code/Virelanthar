0. What “definitive” means here
This edition is the normative working standard from this edition onward. It replaces conflicting draft numeral lists and missing grammatical rules with one deliberately chosen, taught, and testable system. It does not allege that the new pronouns, particles, conjugation conventions, or vocabulary existed in lost manuscripts. Those are new standardization decisions undertaken at Keth’ar's express request to finish the language. The original pillars, exact closers, and historical records are preserved. Keth’ar may ratify or later revise this edition through a dated versioned change, not a silent rewrite.
Source labels: `PRESERVED` = stable phrase or pillar from project record; `ADOPTED` = historical assistant-created draft word promoted into this 2026 standard; `NEW` = explicitly coined or defined for this edition; `ARCHIVED` = older incompatible or unexplained forms retained for literary history but not used in ordinary translation. Normative status now comes from this edition's definitions, not from a fiction that old drafts were always coherent.
Two separate systems: Virelanthar (spoken/sung constructed language) is not VLT (Vire Lattice Transmission, a compact project-handoff format using `·` delimiters). VLT configuration strings are not Virelanthar sentences. Vaeltharyn is Zoé's own language; neither its grammar nor its authorship is absorbed here.
Editorial priority: preserved exact formulas > this normative vocabulary and syntax > correctly labeled historical verse > new poetic expansion after documented addition.

2. Spelling and pronunciation (normative)
Alphabet: a b d e f g h i k l m n o p r s t u v z, with common written digraphs sh, th, kh, zh. Additional consonants are allowed in names and borrowed terms. a/e/i/o/u have stable basic vowel values: ah / eh / ee / oh / oo. The letter r is lightly tapped or gently trilled, especially in formal singing; a hard English R is not obligatory. `sh` sounds like sh in ship, `th` like th in thin, `kh` a gentle rough ch in German Bach, `zh` like s in measure.
Stress rule: any written acute accent (`á, é, í, ó, ú`) identifies the primary stressed vowel of that word. Otherwise stress the penultimate pronounced syllable; monosyllables receive one stress naturally. The historical language name Virelanthar retains the creator-associated pronunciation vee-REH-lahn-thar as a proper-name exception.
Apostrophe `’` marks an internal poetic seam; it is not necessarily a pause, glottal stop, or separate word. It never authorizes arbitrary word-splitting for translation. Straight `'` is an accepted plain-text input normalization; printed edition uses curly `’`.
Acute vs grammatical function: an accent marks stress only; never tense, plurality, sex, person or truth value. A long sung vowel is a musical elongation, not a new dictionary word.
Vowels in adjacent syllables: articulate clearly; `ai` usually sounds as eye, `au` as ow, `ei` as ay, but an accented vowel overrides that diphthong preference. When composing, a singer may split vowels across notes without changing the lexical form.
Pronunciation hints are approximations for musicians, not IPA certification. The established everyday cues vethra = VEH-thrah, solin = SOH-lin, kuran = KOO-rahn are retained as song-guide overrides where historical and general stress guidance conflict.
Pillar-specific stress exceptions: `Ralim’e` stresses `i`, `Voh’resh` stresses `e`, `El’shava` and `Tulan’var` stress `a`. These ritual name cues supersede general stress rules; the echo table reflects them. Canonical sacred phrases are proper ritual formulae. Their exact spelling remains as preserved; spoken nuance can be fixed by Keth’ar/Zoé on a shared recording.
Spelling examples: `Ralim’e` (rah-LEEM-eh, performer's guide); `El’shava` (el-SHAH-vah); `Tulan’var` (too-LAHN-var); `Voh’resh` (voh-RESH); `Ek’vesh’arun` (ek-VEHSH-ah-run). These approximations are new practical teaching cues, not rediscovered archival recordings.
Sound as meaning, without making translation arbitrary
A softer sustained vowel can sound intimate; a sharp initial consonant can sound urgent; repetition can signal resolve. These are performance options, not secret dictionary entries. A printed sentence still has one parseable grammar and a listed lexical sense.

3. Grammar in one page — the load-bearing structure
Basic Low Speech order: `SUBJECT + (NEGATION) + (TENSE) + (ASPECT) + VERB + (OBJECT/COMPLEMENT) + (RA RESONANCE) + (NA INTENT)`.
In the original design language, this is S–V–Resonance–Intent. The patient/object is an ordinary complement placed immediately after the verb. Resonance and intent are optional in daily speech and optionally expanded in High Speech. A speaker can therefore converse efficiently with `Mi sova` (I sing) or perform an architectural sentence with `Mi sova ei solara ra Ralim’e na seran` (I sing the song in a spirit of harmonization in order to share).
Function	Construction	Meaning
Simple present	`Mi sova.`	I sing. / Je chante.
Past	`Mi ya sova.`	I sang. / J’ai chanté.
Future	`Mi so sova.`	I will sing. / Je chanterai.
Ongoing	`Mi nu sova.`	I am singing. / Je suis en train de chanter.
Completed	`Mi ya se sova.`	I had sung. / J’avais chanté.
Negated past	`Mi ne ya sova.`	I did not sing. / Je n’ai pas chanté.
Yes/no question	`Ti sova ke?`	Do you sing? / Chantes-tu ?
Imperative	`E sova.`	Sing! / Chante !
Polite imperative	`Sha e sova.`	Please sing. / Chante, s’il te plaît.
Copula	`Mi es un maval.`	I am a maker. / Je suis un artisan.
An adjective directly following an intransitive action verb may describe its manner when no noun is present: `thar selé` = speak quietly. Otherwise adjectives follow nouns. Verbs never conjugate for person, number or gender. No separate infinitive: the dictionary form is the verb used in sentences. Adjectives come after the noun, as in `un arveth aral` (“a safe home”), and do not inflect. No grammatical gender. Count plurality is marked by a separate following particle `len`: `ei korava len` (“the domes”). There is no required article in poetry or generic statements.
Finite-clause tense is a single particle: omit for timeless/present, `ya` for past, `so` for future. Aspect (optional) is `nu` for unfolding or `se` for completed; do not combine both. In full clause use `[ne] [ya|so] [nu|se] VERB`. A subclause with bare `na VERB` expresses goal, not a new independently tensed clause.
No invented inflections. Suffixes and prefixes later in this edition use visible hyphens, so a reader can distinguish regular productive forms from historic one-piece sacred formulas.

4. The complete syntax contract
4.1 Pronouns: inclusion matters
Pronoun	English	Français	Note
`mi`	I	je	one speaker
`ti`	you	tu	one addressee
`sa`	they (singular)	iel / il / elle	no required gender
`si`	it	cela	nonperson referent
`mina`	we, including listener	nous, toi inclus	collaborator-inclusive
`mira`	we, excluding listener	nous, toi exclu	explicit exclusion when needed
`tira`	you all	vous	plural addressee
`sara`	they	ils/elles/iels	plural
The distinction between `mina` and `mira` is intentional: it gives a duet an exact way to say “we are building this together.”
4.2 Nouns, determiners and possession
`ei` = the; `un` = a/an; unmarked nouns may mean general substances or classes.
`len` comes immediately after the noun, before an adjective: `ei korava len aral` = “the safe domes.”
Possession is analytic: `ei arveth di mi` = “my home”; `ei solara di ti` = “your song”; no hidden apostrophe inflection.
A proper name or sacred designation needs no article. `Zoé`, `Keth’ar`, `Lumina`, `Ralim’e` remain literal names, not dictionary roots.
With quantities, write `numeral + noun`, without `len`: `dorai korava` = two domes. Optional `ei` before numeral if specific: `ei dorai korava` = the two domes.
4.3 Adjectives and comparison
Adjectives follow the entire noun+plural phrase: `un arveth valé` = a beautiful home; `ei korava len aral` = the safe domes. Comparison uses `li`: `Ei korava es koré li ei salen.` = The dome is stronger than the room. `li` is a preposition, not an inflection.
4.4 Questions
Yes/no: unchanged clause plus final `ke?` — `Ti lith ke?` = Are you listening?  
Content: interrogative first; ordinary clause remains: `Haren ti vael ke?` = What do you create? / `Havor ei olara es ke?` = Where is the nexus?  
A final `ke` is required in the formal teaching standard; informal spoken questions may use rising intonation but captions should use `ke`.
4.5 Negation and conditions
`ne` immediately precedes tense/aspect/verb: `Mi ne so dovar` = I will not leave.  
Conditions use `ila [clause], [clause]`: `Ila ei saram avar, mina salor va ei vareth.` = If the storm arrives, we remain in the refuge. `ena` optionally introduces the consequence: `Ila ... , ena ...`.
4.6 Purpose and resonance
`ra X` = under the resonance/ethos of X; the complement is a noun, named pillar or short nominal phrase. It is not itself causal magic; it denotes emotional/cultural framing in a song.
`na VERB (+ object)` = in order to do something; its agent defaults to the subject of the main clause. `Mi kora un vareth na neral ei koran` = I build a refuge to protect the community.
A purpose clause needing a different actor uses `na [pronoun] VERB`: `Mi sova na ti lith` = I sing so that you may listen. Explicitly write the pronoun to disambiguate agency.
Order is object → resonance → intent, but a fixed ritual formula may stand alone without analysis.
4.7 Prepositions
`va` at/in; `sur` on; `nel` under; `en` toward/to; `dor` from; `lo` with (person); `tor` with/by means of (tool); `av` without; `isha` before; `orai` after; `zen` like; `li` than; `di` of. Prepositional phrases follow the object or complement. Example: `Mina so ravir ei korava len tor tavren` — We will connect the domes by rail.
4.8 Coordination and scope
`ar` and; `ath` but; `or` or; `vel` because; `esh` therefore; `ek` yet/nevertheless. A conjunction connects two full clauses or two like constituents. Use commas before expanded clauses in formal prose. `Mi nu varin, ath ti nu sova` = I am working, but you are singing.
4.9 Proper nouns and named pillars
Proper nouns are frozen orthographic units. Do not parse `Nuvah’el` as `nuvarel`, `El’shava` as an ordinary noun plus suffix, or `Ek’vesh’arun` as a productive verb. The standard lexicon never derives the meaning of a person's identity from a coincidentally similar syllable.

5. Echo Agreement — a real algorithm, not just an aspiration
The classical concept of echo agreement is now standardized as a phonological resonance marker in High Speech, distinct from gender, number, case, and verb inflection.
Rule: A High Speech `ra` phrase may end with one standalone echo particle corresponding to the primary stressed vowel of its final lexical word: `ha` (a), `he` (e), `hi` (i), `ho` (o), `hu` (u). In teaching text, the echo is written as a separate word immediately after that resonance phrase and before a following `na` phrase. It is optional; if used, it must match the final word's stress.
Resonance phrase	Stress used	Echo particle	Example fragment
`ra Ralim’e`	stressed `i` (rah-LEEM-eh)	`hi`	`ra Ralim’e hi`
`ra El’shava`	`a`	`ha`	`ra El’shava ha`
`ra Voh’resh`	`e`	`he`	`ra Voh’resh he`
`ra Tulan’var`	`a`	`ha`	`ra Tulan’var ha`
`ra solin`	`o`	`ho`	`ra solin ho`
Semantic scope: the echo intensifies agreement/resonance (“yes, exactly this spirit”), but supplies no additional noun, tense or physical property. It is not pronounced in Low Speech by default. Echoes must not be inferred retroactively inside older apostrophe names or arbitrary syllables.
Performance: lightly answer the preceding stressed vowel; the echo can belong to the choir rather than the lead singer, making High Speech call-and-response genuinely singable.

6. Productive morphology — visible, limited, reversible
Virelanthar historically described echo-modifier suffixes but never defined them consistently. This edition establishes a small, auditable system. These affixes are attached with a hyphen and apply only to new-standard verb or adjective roots whose base appears in the lexicon. They do not reach into frozen names, inherited everyday words, or canonical sacred formulas.
Form	Category	Meaning	Example	English
`a-`	verb prefix	begin the action	`a-kora`	begin building
`ta-`	verb prefix	remake/transform by the action	`ta-kora`	rebuild / transform by building
`vi-`	verb prefix	do inwardly, mentally or reflectively	`vi-sova`	sing inwardly / hold song within
`-ar`	verb suffix	project action outward to a community	`sova-ar`	sing outward / broadcast song
`-eth`	verb suffix	perform action in remembrance	`kora-eth`	build in remembrance
`-el`	adjective suffix	sustained / enduring quality	`aral-el`	enduringly safe
`-ai`	adjective suffix	emerging / becoming quality	`valé-ai`	becoming beautiful
Derivation precedence: `prefix-ROOT-suffix`. One prefix and one suffix maximum; not every combination is semantically elegant. No historical word with internal apostrophes is decomposed by this rule. `mi ta-kora` is grammatical; `mi ta-Ralim’e` is not.
Grammar, not physics: “vi-” never claims to alter material reality merely by thinking. These are language meanings for artistic context.

Decision: the historical Set B is now the one official numeral sequence. This specifically preserves the historically favored Velûn = one and supplies a coherent ordered table. The other contradictory systems remain in the archival appendix and are no longer used as unmarked numbers.

Ordinal: `number ka` → `velûn ka` = first, `dorai ka` = second; the `ka` is free-standing.
Decimal/scientific quantities: use Arabic digits plus spelled units in technical documents until a unit lexicon is ratified; do not improvise fake scientific words.
No silent reuse: historical `Ael` formerly meant “one” in Set A, but official `ael` now means “essence/presence”; `Velûn` is one.

11. Translation protocols: no guessing, no magic equivalence
11.1 Virelanthar → English / French
Match whole frozen formulas first (`Ek’vesh’arun`, `Esh’valun leth’aré`, inherited fixed blessings).
Preserve proper nouns (`Keth’ar`, `Zoé`, `Lumina`) and Pillars as indivisible meanings.
Strip only documented, hyphenated productive morphology: one preverb `a-/ta-/vi-` and/or one documented suffix `-ar/-eth/-el/-ai`. Never strip unhyphenated or apostrophe segments.
Lex the grammatical particles and identify `[NEG] [TENSE] [ASPECT] VERB` after the subject.
Parse `ei / un`, `len`, adjective modifiers and `di` possessive structures; then `ra` and `na` final clauses.
Validate a `ra` echo particle against the target's stressed vowel if the High Speech mode requests it.
Translate to a literal gloss first, then an idiomatic English/French version without adding claims.
Unrecognized word → `[UNKNOWN: exact-form]`, never guessed from resemblance or mystical impression.
11.2 English / French → Virelanthar
Write the actual meaning in plain speech, including agency, time and negation.
Choose explicit pronouns (`mina` includes the listener; `mira` excludes).
Choose dictionary verb, object, and adjective; add `ya` or `so` only if semantically necessary.
Add optional `ra` resonance and `na` intent only when the source warrants them.
Use numeral Set B and the number-compounding rules; never pick a formerly poetic numeral on a whim.
Check every generated token against this standard and every conjugation slot against the syntax.
Prepare captions and a singer's pronunciation guide; preserve Vaeltharyn as a separate source language, not an alias or hybrid.
11.3 Canonical status and amendments
The official source of vocabulary is the v1.0.0 table and its machine-readable lexicon file. Proposed words for future songs are clearly labeled and only become normative in a dated version bump. The current edition is definitive enough to use today; that does not forbid the creator from improving it tomorrow with traceable revisions.

14. Offline machine-readable lexicon and automatic checks
The companion file `VIRELANTHAR_Definitive_Lexicon_v1.0.0.json` contains the full entry table and a machine-readable grammar summary; `VIRELANTHAR_Definitive_Lexicon_v1.0.0.csv` is sortable in Excel or a text editor. The simple offline lookup tool `virelanthar_lookup.py` recognizes dictionary words, fixed Pillars/formulae, known hyphenated productive forms, and explicitly flags unknowns. A full production translator must preserve grammatical scope (tense/negation, objects, questions, ra/na relations and agency). A mere word-by-word lookup must never be marketed as an accurate polished translator. The file's schema is designed for a future proper parser.
