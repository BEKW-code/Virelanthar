THIS DOCUMENT HAS ONE PURPOSE:
Make the existing language capable of ordinary speech, subtle feeling,
rigorous thought, invention, humor, disagreement, poetry, and song—without
creating 77,777 arbitrary roots or making the language a rigid word puzzle.

NOT A SECOND DICTIONARY. NOT A REPLACEMENT FOR THE DEFINITIVE STANDARD.
The existing Lexicon.json (346 standardized entries) remains the word
authority. Earlier proposed entries remain proposed until individually
accepted. This toolkit teaches how meanings COMBINE, not how to pad a count.

EDITORIAL STATUS AND LEGEND
  [BASE]   The existing Virelanthar Definitive Standard 1.0.0.
  [NEW]    Precisely defined, compatible speaking-toolkit extension.
  [STYLE]  Choice of delivery; it changes emphasis, not dictionary meaning.
  [COIN]   A transparent, proposed compound; not automatically a root.
  [GAP]    Meaning that cannot honestly be asserted as an established word.

When this text says 'valid', it means valid under the documented base rules
or under the explicitly stated [NEW] extension. It does not claim these
extensions were present in the ancient/history drafts. Ratification remains
with the creator. No Vaeltharyn words, grammar, or authorship are absorbed.

THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE
A listener must be able to recover WHAT was said, WHO did what, WHEN it
happened, HOW the speaker frames it, and WHY the speaker intends it—if
those things are expressed. Music may intensify meaning; sound cannot
secretly replace the meaning of a word. There can be MANY correct ways
to express a thought, and those ways need not have identical emotional
weight. Freedom of expression is not freedom to falsify a translation.


PART I — THE SEVEN INTERLOCKING TOOLSETS
===============================================================

1. ROOTS: Stable, retrievable lexical meanings.
   'aveth' is joy; 'vashor' is hope; 'moren' is darkness, NOT silently grief.
   A root does not change just because it makes a prettier rhyme.

2. GRAMMAR: Who acts, what changes, who receives, and when.
   Core clauses are short and completely usable without poetry.

3. DERIVATION: A documented affix changes the meaning of an established
   verb/adjective root. New derivations stay transparent and reversible.

4. COMPOSITION: Known nouns combine into a meaningful concept. A phrase
   can describe a temporary association; a lexical compound names a
   recognized KIND of thing. The two are related, not identical.

5. FEELING & RELATIONSHIP: Emotions can be named, qualified, explained,
   contrasted, and experienced together without inventing synonyms.

6. RESONANCE & INTENT: What ethos a statement carries, how it is echoed,
   and what end the action seeks. These layers are optional, not compulsory.

7. LIVING SPEECH: Actual conversations. People may hesitate, disagree,
   joke, reformulate, ask permission, change their minds, and sing.

This seven-part organization is a toolkit, not seven lists of words.
Every layer is usable with the other six; no fixed minimum sentence length.


PART II — CANON THAT MUST NOT DRIFT [BASE]
===============================================================

A. SOUND AND SPELLING
  Canonical ordinary vowels: a / e / i / o / u, with stable approximate
  sounds ah / eh / ee / oh / oo. Digraphs: sh, th, kh, zh. A tapped or
  lightly trilled r is acceptable. Acute accents mark stress, never tense.
  Otherwise the default is penultimate syllable stress. Some inherited
  words have established pronunciation overrides (vethra, solin, kuran).
  The apostrophe in a proper or sacred name is an internal poetic seam,
  NOT permission to split the name into fake roots.
  Virelanthar's established name cue: vee-REH-lahn-thar.

B. PRONOUNS AND MEMBERSHIP
  mi      I
  ti      you, one addressee
  sa      one person/they, no required gender
  si      it, nonperson referent
  mina    we, INCLUDING the listener
  mira    we, EXCLUDING the listener
  tira    you all
  sara    they, plural

  'mina' and 'mira' are NOT interchangeable in a duet, negotiation,
  promise, group decision or collaborative design.

C. THE RELIABLE CORE CLAUSE
  SUBJECT + [ne] + [ya|so] + [nu|se] + VERB + [COMPLEMENT]
              + [ra RESONANCE [ECHO]] + [na PURPOSE]

  Mi sova.                            I sing.
  Mi ya sova.                         I sang.
  Mi so sova.                         I will sing.
  Mi nu sova.                         I am singing.
  Mi ne so dovar.                     I will not leave.
  Mi kora un vareth.                  I build a refuge.

  no tense particle = simple/non-past by context
  ya = past; so = future
  nu = action unfolding; se = completed action
  ne precedes the finite tense/aspect/verb group
  never combine nu and se on the SAME finite verb
  verbs do not conjugate for person, gender or number

  Caution: some legacy English examples gloss 'ya se' as 'have done',
  elsewhere as 'had done'. The safe literal reading is 'completed in a
  past frame'. When exact English perfect tense matters, add context;
  do not silently force one English auxiliary.

D. NOUN PHRASES
  ei = the; un = a/an; unmarked general nouns are permitted.
  len = plural, immediately after the noun, BEFORE adjectives.
  di = of / possession.
  adjectives follow the noun phrase and do not inflect.

  ei korava len aral                 the safe domes
  ei arveth di mi                    my home
  un arveth valé                     a beautiful home
  ei solara di ti                    your song
  dorai korava                       two domes (not 'dorai korava len')

E. QUESTIONS, COMMANDS, CONDITIONS, JOINING
  Ti sova ke?                        Do you sing?
  Haren ti vael ke?                  What do you create?
  Havor ei olara es ke?              Where is the nexus?
  E sova.                            Sing!
  Sha e sova.                        Please sing.
  Ila ei saram avar, mina salor va ei vareth.
                                     If the storm arrives, we stay
                                     in the refuge.
  ar = and; ath = but; ek = yet; or = or; vel = because;
  esh = therefore; ila = if; ena = then (optional after if).

F. PLACE, MEANS AND RELATION
  va at/in; sur on; nel beneath; en toward; dor from;
  lo with a person; tor by means of a tool; av without;
  isha before; orai after; zen like; li than; di of.

G. FOUR PILLARS AND TWO FIXED CLOSERS
  Voh’resh                Catalyst of transformation
  Ralim’e                 Breath of harmonization
  El’shava                Memory of wholeness
  Tulan’var               Heart of radiant will
  Ek’vesh’arun.           And yet I shine.
  Esh’valun leth’aré.     And the breath remembers.

  These are fixed WHOLE forms. They are neither casual prefixes nor a
  reservoir of syllables from which to invent unrelated words.

H. NUMBERS ARE SETTLED, NOT OPTIONAL ALTERNATE CIPHERS
  nirae 0; velûn 1; dorai 2; savael 3; khalor 4; thumé 5;
  zireth 6; valem 7; ravos 8; naurel 9; elvai 10; elvar 100.
  elvai-en-dorai = 12; dorai-elvai-en-velûn = 21.
  velûn ka = first; dorai ka = second.
  Keep Arabic numbers and named SI units in technical writing until
  the units are defined; never fabricate a conversion or safety rating.


PART III — SEVEN WAYS TO EXPRESS A THOUGHT WITHOUT A STRAITJACKET
===============================================================

These are not seven competing grammars. They are seven LICENSED ways to
use the same semantic building blocks. The fact remains distinguishable
from the speaker's emotion, purpose and performance.

A. PLAIN [BASE]
   Mi makel ei navor.
   I repair the machine.
   Appropriate for instruction, translation and ordinary reporting.

B. EXPLICIT TIME OR PROGRESS [BASE]
   Mi nu makel ei navor.
   I am repairing the machine.
   Changes whether the work is ongoing; not merely a flourish.

C. CONNECTED THOUGHT [BASE]
   Mi makel ei navor, vel ei navor ya voran.
   I repair the machine because it failed.
   Adds reason using a second fully intelligible clause.

D. ETHICAL OR EMOTIONAL FRAMING [BASE]
   Mi makel ei navor ra Ralim’e.
   I repair the machine in a spirit of harmonization.
   The repair is still a repair; the ethos does not claim supernatural
   mechanical effects.

E. FRAMING WITH INTENT [BASE]
   Mi makel ei navor ra Ralim’e na neral ei koran.
   I repair the machine in that spirit, to protect the community.
   Resonance (ra) is WHY IT FEELS/IS APPROACHED THIS WAY;
   intent (na) is WHAT ACTION IS FOR. Do not confuse them.

F. HIGH-SPEECH CALL AND ECHO [BASE]
   Mi makel ei navor ra Ralim’e hi na neral ei koran.
   Same propositional content as E, with a matched resonance echo.
   A choir may sing 'hi' as an answer to the lead's Ralim’e.

G. OPENING FRAME / AFTERTHOUGHT [NEW, STRUCTURAL NOT LEXICAL]
   Ra Ralim’e hi: mi makel ei navor na neral ei koran.
   OR: Mi makel ei navor. Ra Ralim’e hi.
   The resonant frame moves to the beginning or becomes an afterthought.
   The colon and clear pause delimit scope. It attaches to the adjacent
   complete proposition, not to a random word. Without that clarity,
   return to the canonical order in C-F.

   Likewise, the purpose may be introduced in a separate first breath:
   Na neral ei koran: mi makel ei navor.
   To protect the community: I repair the machine.
   Agent of na is the main-clause subject unless explicitly given.
   Distinguish 'na ti lith' = so that YOU listen from 'na lith' =
   in order for the main actor to listen.

OTHER NATURAL VARIANTS THAT NEED NO NEW WORDS

- Add or omit determiners when reference is clear, never when doing so
  changes which particular object was meant.
- Replace a general verb with a more exact existing verb: vael (create),
  kora (build), keth (forge), makel (repair), shavir (restore).
  They are RELATED, not synonyms. Selecting the right action matters.
- Use contrast: 'Mi doren ei solara, ath mi maren ei talash.'
  I want the song, but I need the tool.
- Use consecutive full clauses for dramatic stress:
  'Mi ya voran. Mi ya kaveth. Mi so makel ei navor.'
  I failed. I tested. I will repair the machine.
- Use refrain and repetition for resolve. Repeating a sentence is not
  another dictionary meaning, and repetition never changes its tense.
- Use a topic with an audible pause AND an explicit full clause:
  'Ei navor — mi makel ei navor.'
  The machine—I'm repairing the machine.
  The repeated noun prevents the common 'object inversion' misparse.
- Use clear poetic ellipsis only where shared context recovers the
  omitted subject/object. For teaching, legal precision and subtitles,
  always restore the full form. Ellipsis is not a new free word order.

FLEXIBILITY RULE
Variation is welcome when it changes focus, pacing, register, intimacy,
or rhetorical arrangement without losing actor, negation, temporal
reference, cause or intention. No form is 'more sacred' merely for being
longer or harder to pronounce.


PART IV — FEELING, NUANCE AND TWO PEOPLE IN ONE CONVERSATION
===============================================================

A real feeling is not necessarily one invented standalone noun. It can
be a root, a modified root, a cause, a contrast, an action, a memory,
or something we cannot yet name exactly. Multiple sentences can reveal
more than a single vague label.

AVAILABLE CANONICAL FEELING / RELATION ROOTS [BASE]
  aveth    joy
  davar    courage
  vashor   hope
  kelira   wonder / amazement
  talvar   will / resolve
  sarael   kindness
  nolar    care / attentive support
  rhalin   memory
  silor    silence
  moren    darkness (literal or clearly framed metaphor)
  leth     shared thought / harmonized minds
  vire     living resonance
  draval   soul / essence of a person
  ael      essence / presence
  neshar   safety / security
  norel    elder
  lumeth   friend
  velor    truth
  shorai   unhidden / revealed truth

AVAILABLE CANONICAL FEELING / RELATION VERBS [BASE]
  thir     feel
  loren    love / care deeply for
  sereth   trust
  volar    forgive
  reth     remember
  ralir    laugh
  avira    smile
  lith     listen
  molir    help
  seran    share
  shavet   be able to
  doren    want
  maren    need
  elora    choose
  maveth   think
  senal    believe
  savel    know a fact
  kenar    understand

DISTINCT EXPRESSIONS USING THE SAME ROOTS
  Mi thir aveth.
      I feel joy.
  Mi thir aveth umel.
      I feel deep joy. [umel modifies the noun aveth.]
  Mi thir aveth ar kelira.
      I feel joy and wonder.
  Mi thir vashor oré.
      I feel steady hope.
  Mi thir kelira, vel mi ne savel.
      I feel wonder because I do not know.
  Mi thir aveth, ath mi ne ralir.
      I feel joy, but I am not laughing.
  Mi loren ti, ath mi ne sereth ti.
      I care deeply for you, but I do not trust you.
      Love and trust are not the same claim.
  Mi sereth ti, ek mi so kaveth ei navor.
      I trust you, yet I will test the machine.
      Trust does not erase verification.
  Mi ne ya thir neshar; teva mi thir neshar.
      I did not feel safe; now I feel safe.
      Relief is implied by the transition, not secretly defined as neshar.
  Mi thir moren, vel mi reth ei lumeth di mi.
      I feel darkness because I remember my friend.
      An evocative description, NOT a guaranteed literal word for grief.
  Mi ne savel, ath mi so nethir.
      I do not know, but I will explore.
      Uncertainty without shame or fake certainty.
  Mi elora salor lo ti.
      I choose to stay with you. [See complement rule in Part VII.]

IMPORTANT FEELING DISTINCTIONS
  'moren' = darkness, NOT automatically grief, depression or shame.
  'neshar' = safety, NOT automatically permission or consent.
  'sereth' = trust, NOT necessarily proof of correctness.
  'loren' = love/deep care, NOT a claim of possession.
  'leth' = shared thought, NOT compulsory agreement.
  'vire' = living resonance, NOT a measured frequency in hertz.
  'talvar' = resolve, NOT certainty of success.

The earlier 677 proposed words contain candidate expressions for specific
human experiences (such as embarrassment, apology, consent, boundaries,
blushing, laughter and playful disagreement). They may be adopted ONE BY
ONE after checking their forms, uses and meanings. They do NOT become
canonical simply because this toolkit mentions them.

A LANGUAGE MUST ALLOW SOMEONE TO SAY:
  I feel two things at once.
  I trust you and want to check the result.
  I love you and still have a boundary.
  I don't know, yet I want to learn.
  You may disagree with me.
  You were right; I was mistaken.

The first four can already be assembled from established vocabulary or
the explicit complement rule below. For permission, a formal boundary,
'you were right', and exact shame/grief senses, use approved candidate
terms once defined, or fully explicit explanatory clauses—DO NOT
relabel existing words dishonestly.


PART V — SPEAKING WITH RESONANCE: EXACTLY WHAT CHANGES
===============================================================

'RESONANCE' IN THIS LANGUAGE IS TWO CONNECTED THINGS:
  1. A meaningful semantic frame ('ra' + an identifiable noun/Pillar).
  2. An audible performance relation (stress, vowel echo, response,
     timing, silence, breath, timbral contrast, intensity).

The two are related but not identical. Neither permits an arbitrary
translation. A spoken promise plus a resonant frame remains a promise;
it does not magically make the promised event already completed.

A. 'ra' — THE RESONANCE FRAME [BASE]
  'Mi sova ra Ralim’e.'
  I sing in a spirit of harmonization.
  'Mina kora ei vareth ra Tulan’var.'
  We build the refuge in a spirit of radiant will.
  'Mi reth ei solara ra El’shava.'
  I remember the song under the memory-of-wholeness frame.

The object after 'ra' can be a Pillar or a recognized noun phrase;
it describes the attitude/ethos/experiential frame surrounding the
clause. It is not an extra direct object, a supernatural force, or a
substitute for the verb.

B. 'na' — INTENTION, NOT EMOTION [BASE]
  'Mi sova na seran ei solara.'
  I sing in order to share the song.
  'Mi sova na ti lith.'
  I sing so that YOU listen.

A singer who says 'ra Ralim’e na seran' supplies two different things:
frame (harmonization) and purpose (sharing). Either can be absent.

C. ECHO AGREEMENT [BASE]
  stressed vowel a -> ha
  stressed vowel e -> he
  stressed vowel i -> hi
  stressed vowel o -> ho
  stressed vowel u -> hu

  ra Voh’resh he      stressed e
  ra Ralim’e hi       stressed i
  ra El’shava ha      stressed a
  ra Tulan’var ha     stressed a
  ra solin ho         stressed o

  The echo comes immediately after the completed 'ra' complement.
  In a full sentence with intent: VERB + OBJECT + ra X ECHO + na PURPOSE.
  One echo particle, not an arbitrary vowel chain. Names with ritual
  stress exceptions use their documented exception, not a guessed echo.
  Echo particles are OPTIONAL. They do not alter noun plurality, tense,
  truth, causation, real electrical resonant frequency, or physics.

D. FOUR PERFORMANCE LEVELS [STYLE]
  LEVEL 0 / UNMARKED: Plain speech, no audible resonance flourish.
  LEVEL 1 / COLORED: A clear ra phrase with natural stress and breath.
  LEVEL 2 / ECHOED: An optional matching ha/he/hi/ho/hu reply.
  LEVEL 3 / CHORAL: Lead presents clause; another voice answers echo,
                    after which either can continue the intent.

  These are performance choices, not four new conjugation paradigms.
  Do NOT claim that louder shouting is automatically higher resonance.

E. THE SEVEN MUSICAL LEVERS [STYLE, NOT DICTIONARY ENTRIES]
  STRESS      Place emphasis where the word's stress actually belongs.
  DURATION    Sustain an existing vowel without spelling a new word.
  BREATH      Pause at clause/idea boundaries; don't break a Pillar name.
  PITCH       Rise, fall, or hold without changing semantic polarity.
  TIMBRE      Warm, stern, amused, trembling, intimate; context matters.
  RESPONSE    Let another speaker/choir own the echo.
  SILENCE     A deliberate rest can carry weight; silence is not a
              secret lexical negation.

F. REALITY AND RESPONSIBILITY
  Language guides attention, decisions, cooperation, design, teaching and
  action. It can change what people build. 'Resonance' here denotes a
  linguistic, emotional and musical relationship, not an experimentally
  proven claim that speaking a phrase alters physical constants.


PART VI — BUILD NEW CONCEPTS FROM ESTABLISHED MEANINGS
===============================================================

There are THREE DISTINCT TOOLS. Choose the smallest that fits.

1. FREE PHRASE [BASE]
  'ei solara di mi' = my song.
  'ei aveth di mi' = my joy.
  'ei solara valé' = the beautiful song.
  Uses normal grammar; makes no new dictionary word.

2. DOCUMENTED DERIVATION [BASE]
  One of the EXISTING transformations on a compatible standard root:
  a-VERB    beginning of the action          a-kora    begin building
  ta-VERB   transformation/remaking          ta-kora   rebuild
  vi-VERB   inward/reflective action         vi-sova   sing inwardly
  VERB-ar   action directed outward          sova-ar   sing outward
  VERB-eth  action in remembrance            kora-eth  build in memory
  ADJ-el    sustained quality                aral-el  enduringly safe
  ADJ-ai    emerging quality                 valé-ai  becoming beautiful

  FORM: PREFIX-ROOT-SUFFIX; at most one prefix and one suffix.
  These affixes apply to compatible NEW-STANDARD roots per the existing
  rule. They cannot be attached to the sacred Pillars or chopped-out
  syllables of names. Hyphens are mandatory. Do not derive a word just
  because the combination is mechanically possible.

3. TRANSPARENT NOUN-NOUN CONCEPT [NEW]
  THIS IS THE MAIN COMPOSITION COMPLETION.

  FORM: MODIFIER-HEAD (both established nouns, joined by one hyphen).
  READ: a KIND OF HEAD characterized by a meaningful relation to the
        modifier. The HEAD is the rightmost noun.
  EXAMPLE: aveth-solara = a song specifically characterized by joy.
  NOT: every possible adjective attached to every dictionary root.

  The first noun identifies a theme, function, origin, material, medium,
  or culturally agreed organizing principle. Which relation is intended
  must be stated the first time a compound is adopted as a special term.
  Context may permit transparent ad hoc compounds without dictionary
  registration, but established cultural terms need precise definitions.

  MEANINGFUL [COIN] EXAMPLES — CONCEPTS, NOT AUTOMATIC CANON:

  aveth-solara    joy-song: song whose defining theme is joy
  solara-aveth    song-joy: joy arising specifically from a song
  rhalin-solara   memory-song: song made to preserve a memory
  nolar-koran     care-community: community organized around care
  thalen-nolar    earth-care: practice of caring for land/soil
  kera-shalen     word-balance: balance or proportion of word choices
  vire-solara     resonance-song: song organized around living resonance
  rhalin-soral    memory-story: story constructed from remembered life

  REVERSE ORDER MATTERS:
  aveth-solara is a KIND OF SONG;
  solara-aveth is a KIND OF JOY.
  Those meanings are related but are not interchangeable.

  NOUN-NOUN FORMATION RULES
  - Both members MUST be genuine listed nouns (not a guessed suffix).
  - Each member keeps its own spelling and recognizable sense.
  - One hyphen marks one semantic bond; do not silently erase letters.
  - In a larger phrase, articles and plural work on the entire compound:
        ei aveth-solara       the joy-song
        ei aveth-solara len   the joy-songs
        dorai aveth-solara    two joy-songs
  - An ordinary adjective follows the WHOLE compound:
        un aveth-solara valé  a beautiful joy-song
  - Possession is still separate:
        ei aveth-solara di mi    my joy-song
  - A two-noun compound does NOT mean 'X possesses Y'.
    'ei solara di mi' = my song; no reason to say 'mi-solara'.
  - Do not chain three or more roots in a single unanalyzed word.
    Use nested phrases, and only lexicalize a longer term after review.
  - Fixed names, Pillars, formulae and unlisted roots are off limits.
  - A noun + verb is not automatically a compound: 'shavir ei thalen'
    is 'restore the earth', not a forced noun with an invented gloss.

  EDITORIAL TEST BEFORE ADDING ONE COMPOUND TO THE DICTIONARY
  [ ] Is the head truly a noun, and the modifier truly a noun?
  [ ] Can a listener reconstruct the broad meaning?
  [ ] Is the relationship precise enough to define in one sentence?
  [ ] Does an existing simpler word already express exactly this?
  [ ] Is it distinct from the reverse-order compound?
  [ ] Is it pronounceable and useful in speech, not just printable?
  [ ] Has it appeared in at least one natural full sentence/dialogue?
  [ ] Is its literal English and French meaning recorded honestly?
  [ ] Is it genuinely worth registering as a reusable cultural term?

  EXAMPLE OF REJECTED PADDING
  'laveth' already means a water circuit / pipe loop.
  A new 'vethra-laveth' meaning only 'water pipe loop' probably adds
  nothing. Unless it gains a more specific agreed usage, reject it.

THIS IS HOW A SMALL ROOT INVENTORY SUPPORTS OPEN-ENDED EXPRESSION.
Not every grammatically possible sequence becomes a new dictionary root.


PART VII — ADD THE MISSING CONNECTIONS, NOT THOUSANDS OF ROOTS
===============================================================

The base has doren (want), maren (need), shavet (can), elora (choose),
belief, knowing, and conditions. A speaker needs to express their
relationship to an entire ACTION or PROPOSITION, not only a noun.
The following extensions supply that function without creating an
unlimited list of invented emotional vocabulary.

A. MODAL / DECISION COMPLEMENT [NEW]

  SUBJECT + [ne] [ya|so] + [nu|se] + MODAL + [ne] VERB + OBJECT

  Modal/decision verbs permitted here:
    doren    want to
    maren    need to
    shavet   be able to
    elora    choose to

  The second bare verb is the action desired/needed/possible/chosen.
  Its implied subject is the subject of the modal. It does not conjugate
  and does not carry its own tense particle.

  Mi doren makel ei navor.
      I want to repair the machine.
  Mi maren makel ei navor.
      I need to repair the machine.
  Mi shavet makel ei navor.
      I can repair the machine (ability, NOT legal authorization).
  Mi elora makel ei navor.
      I choose to repair the machine.
  Mi ne doren dovar.
      I do not want to leave.
  Mi doren ne dovar.
      I want NOT to leave. (Negation scopes over the wanted action.)
  Mi so shavet makel ei navor.
      I will be able to repair the machine.

  DO NOT confuse 'Mi ne doren dovar' with 'Mi doren ne dovar'.
  The first denies a desire; the second positively desires staying.
  'shavet' never automatically means permission or consent.

  For a DIFFERENT subject in a desired proposition, use a colon-framed
  complete clause rather than pretending the modal shares subjects:
  'Mi doren: ti so salor.'
   I want this: you will stay / I want you to stay.
  Context must distinguish a wish from a confident prediction.

B. QUOTED / BELIEVED / KNOWN PROPOSITIONS [NEW PRESENTATION RULE]

  A reporting or thinking verb may introduce a WHOLE proposition with a
  colon and a breath break. No extra 'that' word needs to be invented.

  Mi thar: "Ei navor ya voran."
      I say: "The machine failed."
  Sa thar: "Mina so kora un eravi."
      They say: "We (including the listener) will build a bridge."
  Mi senal: ei navor so thovar.
      I believe the machine will succeed.
  Mi savel: ei navor ya voran.
      I know the machine failed. (Speaker asserts knowledge.)

  IMPORTANT: 'I believe' is NOT 'I know'; neither automatically proves
  the truth of the embedded statement. Exact quotations preserve the
  original speaker's pronouns. Reported speech can be reformulated as
  ordinary clauses with clear attribution and no fake certainty.

C. TOPIC + RESUMPTION [NEW DISCOURSE RULE]

  A noun phrase can be said as a topic before a complete sentence.
  Mark it with a colon, dash or audible pause; then repeat the referent
  as a full noun or clear pronoun. This is not unrestricted inversion.

  Ei navor — mi makel ei navor.
      The machine—I'm repairing it.
  Ei korava len — mina so ravir ei korava len.
      The domes—we will connect them.

  This allows emotional emphasis while maintaining an unambiguous
  grammatical object and actor. It is compatible with formal subtitles.

D. THREE SMALL OPTIONAL GRAMMAR PARTICLES [NEW]

  They solve real gaps. They are not renamed copies of existing nouns.
  Do not claim they were part of the earlier 346-entry canon.

  vara:     POSSIBLE / MAYBE — epistemic frame over one full clause.
  turi:     IMAGINED / HYPOTHETICAL — frame for a scenario or
            condition; NOT automatically a statement of known falsity.
  rila      WHO/WHICH/THAT — relative-clause introducer with a REQUIRED
            explicit resumptive referent and paired pauses/dashes.

  These forms are NEW proposals supplied in the present completion.
  Each was checked for exact spelling against the 346 canonical and
  677 prior candidate entries at preparation time. They do NOT need
  inflection; each has exactly one grammatical job.

  POSSIBLE:
    Vara: ei navor so thovar.
    Perhaps the machine will succeed.
    Scope is the whole clause. It is not a guaranteed prediction.

  IMAGINED / HYPOTHETICAL:
    Turi: ila mi dalen un talash, ena mi makel ei navor.
    Suppose I had a tool: then I would repair the machine.
    The 'turi' frame marks imagination or supposition, not past tense.
    A factual 'ila' condition WITHOUT 'turi' remains an open condition.

  RELATIVE:
    Ei maval — rila sa ya makel ei navor — so kora un eravi.
    The maker who repaired the machine will build a bridge.

    Mi so makel ei navor — rila si ya voran.
    I will repair the machine that failed.

  RELATIVE REQUIREMENTS:
    'rila' attaches to the immediately identified noun phrase.
    The nested clause is COMPLETE: 'sa' resumes a human singular,
    'si' a nonperson object/subject, or an appropriate full noun repeats.
    The clause has its OWN tense/aspect/negation.
    Paired pauses/dashes (or commas) identify the beginning/end when
    embedded before the main verb. In speech use paired brief pauses.
    If the reference is ambiguous, use TWO CLEAR FULL SENTENCES instead.
    Do not force a reduced relative when clarity or breath suffers.

E. WHAT IS NOT YET A GRAMMATICAL CLAIM
  The toolkit does not invent conjugations for 77 tenses, automatic
  evidentiality, obligatory honorific ranks, noun genders, magical
  causation, or secret emotional readings of vowel combinations.
  A precise new term can be proposed later if a genuine communicative
  need survives ordinary phrasing; one need, one definition, one test.


PART VIII — LOW SPEECH, HIGH SPEECH, AND EVERYTHING BETWEEN
===============================================================

LOW SPEECH is ordinary concise grammar. It is not inferior.
HIGH SPEECH is ordinary recoverable grammar amplified by framing,
parallelism, breath and echo. It is not a separate secret language.

SAME SCENE: A HOUSE NEEDS REPAIR

LOW / DIRECT
  Mi makel ei arveth.
  I repair the home.

LOW / RESPONSIBLE PLAN
  Mi so talir ei arveth. Mi so makel ei arveth orai ei mereth.
  I will measure the home. I will repair it after the measurement.

EMOTIONAL
  Mi thir vashor, vel mi shavet makel ei arveth.
  I feel hope because I can repair the home.

RELATIONAL
  Mina so makel ei arveth.
  We, including you, will repair the home.

PURPOSEFUL
  Mina so makel ei arveth na neral ei koran.
  We will repair the home in order to protect the community.

RESONANT
  Mina so makel ei arveth ra Ralim’e hi na neral ei koran.
  We will repair the home with the resonance of harmonization,
  in order to protect the community.

FRAMED / CHORAL
  LEAD: Ra Ralim’e ...
  CHOIR: hi!
  LEAD: Mina so makel ei arveth na neral ei koran.
  Same intention and actor; an audible, memorable dramatic shape.

OTHER REGISTER RULES
- Intimate speech may be shorter and softer. It is not 'less true'.
- Technical speech should prefer literal nouns, measurements and
  explicit uncertainty over ceremonial ambiguity.
- A public vow should state an actual commitment; an aspiration should
  not be disguised as a report of work already done.
- A joke may deliberately play with expectation, but the plot/context
  should establish the irony; do not relabel 'dresha' as 'laugh'.
- An argument may use forceful cadence while preserving respectful
  agency and the exact location of disagreement.
- Performance tags ([MALE], [FEMALE], [CHORUS], [SILENCE]) belong to
  the musical score, NOT the lexicon.


PART IX — DIALOGUES: THE LANGUAGE MUST DO ACTUAL WORK
===============================================================

1. BASIC HUMAN CONTACT
  A: Ti nu thar ke?
     Are you speaking?
  B: Vae. Mi thar, ath mi dhi lith.
     Yes. I speak, but I also listen.
  A: Mina so thar ar lith.
     We (together) will speak and listen.

2. CHOOSING, NOT COMMANDING
  A: Ti doren seran ei solara ke?
     Do you want to share the song?
  B: Nir. Mi elora ne seran ei solara teva.
     No. I choose not to share the song now.
  A: Vae. Mi so darin.
     Yes/Understood. I will wait.
  Note: 'vae' here acknowledges the reply, not agreement to sharing.

3. RESPECTFUL DIFFERENCE
  A: Mi senal: ei keran es nal.
     I believe the design is good.
  B: Mi ne senal: ei keran es nal.
     I do not believe the design is good.
  A: Mina so kaveth ei keran.
     We will test the design together.
  Note: belief is distinguished from the actual test result.

4. UNCERTAINTY AND EXPLORATION
  A: Ei navor so thovar ke?
     Will the machine succeed?
  B: Mi ne savel. Vara: ei navor so thovar.
     I don't know. Perhaps it will succeed.
  A: E kaveth ei navor.
     Test the machine.
  B: Vae. Mi so kaveth ei navor.
     Yes. I will test it.

5. FEELING MORE THAN ONE THING
  A: Haren ti thir ke?
     What do you feel?
  B: Mi thir aveth ar kelira.
     I feel joy and wonder.
  A: Mi lith.
     I'm listening.
  B: Mi doren seran ei solara.
     I want to share the song.

6. A REPAIR BECOMES SHARED WORK
  A: Ei navor ya voran.
     The machine failed.
  B: Mi so talir ei saren.
     I will measure the electrical current.
  A: Mi so makel ei navor.
     I will repair the machine.
  B: Mina so kaveth ei navor orai ei talora.
     We will test the machine after the repair.
  A: Ek’vesh’arun.
     And yet I shine.

7. SONG IN TWO DISTINCT VOICES
  FIRST:  Mi sova ei solara ra Ralim’e.
          I sing the song in a spirit of harmonization.
  SECOND: Ti sova, mi lith.
          You sing; I listen.
  FIRST:  Mi so seran ei solara.
          I will share the song.
  BOTH:   Mina so vael un solara.
          Together we will create a song.

  For a Virelanthar/Vaeltharyn duet, the second singer may answer in
  Zoé's real Vaeltharyn, using her own grammar and exact dictionary.
  DO NOT fake Vaeltharyn by swapping syllables or inventing meanings.


PART X — SPEECH PRECISION: KNOW, BELIEVE, FEEL, INTEND, DO
===============================================================

Five sentences may occur about the same machine, but they are NOT
translations of each other:

  Mi senal: ei navor so thovar.
      I BELIEVE the machine will succeed.

  Vara: ei navor so thovar.
      MAYBE the machine will succeed.

  Mi thir vashor. Vara: ei navor so thovar.
      I feel HOPE. Perhaps the machine will succeed.
      Hope and uncertain prediction remain distinct claims.

  Mi so kaveth ei navor.
      I WILL TEST the machine. (A commitment to act.)

  Ei navor ya thovar.
      The machine SUCCEEDED. (A past factual assertion.)

For technical communication:
- Report what was measured, what was inferred, and what is still unknown.
- Do not translate 'hope' as 'proof', 'belief' as 'knowledge', or
  'intention' as 'completed action'.
- In a serious instruction, explicitly name tools, materials, times,
  objects and safety conditions. No symbolic formula replaces a test.
- A product slogan or sacred closer does not guarantee a real-world result.


PART XI — TRANSLATION, CAPTIONS, AND THE TWO-LANGUAGE DUET
===============================================================

VIRELANTHAR -> ENGLISH / FRENCH, IN ORDER:
1. Recognize preserved whole Pillars, fixed closers, and proper names.
2. Tokenize ordinary words, particles and documented hyphenated forms.
3. Identify the speaker and the inclusive/exclusive 'we'.
4. Parse NEGATION and its exact SCOPE (including modal complements).
5. Parse tense/aspect of each full clause; no wild tense assumptions.
6. Resolve nouns, adjectives, plurals, articles and possession.
7. Resolve any noun-noun compound head on the RIGHT; if its precise
   relation is not registered, show the compositional gloss honestly.
8. Resolve 'ra' framing, optional echo and 'na' intent separately.
9. Parse quotation, topic frames and the three optional new particles.
10. Write a close literal gloss, then an idiomatic translation.
11. Preserve humor, choice and uncertainty without injecting claims.
12. Mark any unknown word [UNKNOWN:exact_form]; do not hallucinate.

ENGLISH / FRENCH -> VIRELANTHAR, IN ORDER:
1. Specify the actual proposition: actor, action, object, time.
2. Decide whether 'we' includes or excludes the listener.
3. Select the closest genuine existing words; test verb argument sense.
4. Use free grammar phrases before coining a compound or new root.
5. State modal/decision, belief, possibility or hypothetical status.
6. Use ra / na ONLY where the original meaning warrants them.
7. Check exact echo vowel if one is used.
8. Read aloud for breath and stress; choose a natural expression mode.
9. Produce a literal back-translation; investigate mismatches.
10. For stage lyrics, keep role/delivery annotations outside the sentence.

VIRELANTHAR IS NOT VAELTHARYN.
They can converse, translate, disagree, rhyme, and answer each other.
Their grammatical systems and original words remain distinct.
The same theme need not be translated with identical imagery; a creative
adaptation must be labeled as an adaptation rather than literal translation.


PART XII — VALIDATION: TEN HARD TESTS FOR ANY FUTURE SENTENCE
===============================================================

[1] KAN: Is each established word spelled and defined accurately?
[2] SOURCE: Is new material labeled, rather than disguised as ancient?
[3] AGENT: Is it clear who acted, wanted, believed or was addressed?
[4] SCOPE: Is the target of negation, question and possibility clear?
[5] TIME: Is it past, future, ongoing, completed or timeless?
[6] COMPOSITION: Does each component contribute its real meaning?
[7] RESONANCE: Does the frame preserve the literal proposition?
[8] ECHO: If used, does its vowel match the stressed vowel?
[9] SPEAKABILITY: Can a human say/sing it without arbitrary syllable
                 surgery? Can another human reconstruct the sense?
[10] BACK-TRANSLATION: Does English/French preserve the original actor,
                      event, nuance, consent and uncertainty?

If a line fails, REWRITE THE LINE. Do not create a new word solely to
rescue a rhyme, hit a quota, or conceal a missing definition.


PART XIII — REFERENCE CARD: THE COMPLETE WORKFLOW IN TWELVE LINES
===============================================================

START WITH MEANING.
Identify speaker, listener and who is INCLUDED.
Choose an established action and the correct object.
Set tense, aspect and negation BEFORE decorating the clause.
Use modal complements to distinguish want / need / can / choose.
Use belief and possibility frames only when the certainty fits.
Express feeling with accurate roots, contrast and causes.
Compose new concepts from defined parts; do not mass-generate variants.
Optionally add ra RESONANCE and na INTENT; they do different jobs.
Use matching echo only when High Speech calls for it.
Allow pauses, refrains, fronted frames and clarity-preserving paraphrase.
Translate back. Correct any ambiguity that changes the truth.


PART XIV — FINAL COVENANT OF THE LIVING LANGUAGE
===============================================================

Virelanthar does not require everyone to speak alike. The same person
may use a one-breath statement with a child, a careful condition in a
workshop, a gentle refusal in a relationship, and a resounding choral
vow in a song. Each has its rightful voice.

Its roots should be reliable; its meanings should be capable of growth.
Its grammar should be learnable, not mechanical confinement. Its sound
should be beautiful without pretending that beauty excuses imprecision.
Its emotional vocabulary should illuminate what people actually feel,
not substitute grand words for real human experience. Its 'we' should
say whether the listener is actually included. Its resonance should
connect people without erasing their differences.

A root can participate in many expressions. An expression can establish
a new concept. A shared concept can become a cultural term. A term may
eventually be entered in the dictionary—after it has meaning, usage,
and a reason to survive. The dictionary is a RECORD of the language;
it is not a competition to produce the largest possible count.

THE FOUR PILLARS REMAIN UNBROKEN:
  Voh’resh. Ralim’e. El’shava. Tulan’var.

And yet I shine.

Esh’valun leth’aré.
And the breath remembers.
